Where the names came from

A theme the manager likes

The names were picked in one chat on 5 October and pruned on 13 October. Each step kept what already existed and added a plain label to everything new.

5 October · the givens

Three names already existed

In the chat titled “Product name suggestions”, Venkatesh explained that his manager likes the Transformers series, that the team is called Wheeljack and that Optimus Logger is already part of the team's naming. He wanted a Teams bot called @b, “bee as in bumblebee”.

5 October · candidates

Nine names for the whole platform

Nexus, Ark, Prime, Forge, Cybertron, AllSpark, Matrix, Teletraan and Energon were all considered as the overall name. They were alternatives, not extra products, and four of them were never used again.

5 October · the hierarchy

Cybertron, then Vector Sigma, then aliases

The proposal put Project Cybertron over the platform, Vector Sigma as the platform and @B as its conversational entry point. AllSpark lost the top spot and was kept for AI creation. Each capability got an alias with a metaphor beside its job.

13 October · Venkatesh's decisions

Five renames, eight kept

Venkatesh renamed the platform and four capabilities to plainer names: Vector Sigma became Vector, Teletraan became CodeX, Space Bridge became Bridge, Energon became Compute and Perceptor became Evals. Matrix, AllSpark, GroundBridge, Sentinel, Optimus Logger, Vault, Ratchet and @B stayed.

The tagline

One platform, from code to production

The proposal's tagline is “One platform. Every application. From code to production.” The handbook treats it as positioning. The prototype has one complete scenario, and the v3 story ends with a promotion to QA waiting for its second signature.

Rules and traps

Five ways a name misleads

A themed name is only safe next to its plain label. These are the five places where the handbook and the v3 screens show it going wrong, and what each one asks you to say instead.

Trap 1 · Vault

One word, three things

On the v3 screens “Vault” is the artifact registry in the sidebar, the vault:secret/dev/commerce secret path in every environment strip, and the HashiCorp Vault connector in Bridge. When two of them meet, say “artifact registry” or “credential vault”.

Trap 2 · Matrix

Not a permissions matrix

Matrix is the enterprise graph of applications, services and their evidence. The table of who may run experiments or approve a promotion lives on the Sentinel screen. Both look like a matrix to a newcomer.

Trap 3 · Transformers

The theme, not the model

Transformers is the naming theme, taken from the series the manager likes. The transformer is a neural-network architecture. The prototype implements neither a transformer model nor model training.

Trap 4 · Wheeljack

“Deploy Wheeljack” is a question

Wheeljack is a team, so the request names no application, version or environment. On the v3 @B screen, @B answers with a question and offers vector-shell and teams-bee, the two repositories v3 ties to Wheeljack.

Trap 5 · identifiers

Branding changes, identifiers stay

A rebrand of the prototype changes the title, logo, metadata, story copy and accessible labels together. The browser key atlas-demo-v3, AtlasProvider, component folders and route slugs stay unless someone decides a migration.

five traps · what to say
  1. Name beside a labelIntroduce a themed name with what it does: “Matrix, the enterprise graph”. Nobody should need Transformers knowledge.
  2. Use the real screen labelWhen describing the prototype, use its labels, such as Workbench, and add the alias in parentheses.
  3. One name per levelProject Cybertron for the initiative, Vector for the platform, Atlas for the prototype's code and screens.
  4. Write @B (bee)Keep @b when quoting the existing plan. A real bot must resolve its verified mention, not match the text.
  5. Wheeljack is a team“Deploy Wheeljack” stays ambiguous until an application, artifact and environment are named.
  6. Optimus Logger existsIt is a named team tool. The prototype has no endpoint, schema or ownership contract for it.
  7. Identifiers stayatlas-demo-v3, component folders and route slugs change only with a migration decision.

The seven conventions of handbook 1.6, with rule 3 updated from Vector Sigma to Vector.

How the prototype does it

Atlas, everywhere

The working prototype predates every naming decision. Its screens use plain labels, and its code is named Atlas from the browser key down.

The prototype's 22 views carry plain labels such as Workbench, Enterprise graph, Playground and Releases. The handbook's operating chapters use those labels so that a reader can follow them without the crosswalk, and add a themed name in parentheses only where it helps.

Routes come from the view names: the label is lowercased and spaces become hyphens, so Traces & usage lives at /#traces-&-usage. Renaming a view therefore renames its route, and old links stop working unless someone keeps an alias.

The handbook draws a line between two layers. A rebrand decides the display layer together: title and logo, metadata, story copy, accessible labels, downloadable references and README. The identifier layer stays put. If the persistence key ever changes, someone must decide whether to migrate, retain or reset the records saved under the old one.

Where the names sit on screen

Three v3 boards carry the names: the sidebar on every screen, the Architecture board that maps them to planes, and @B, where a name causes a real question. Select a plate to open the full screen.

Plate 02.1 · every screen's sidebar

Themed names under plain headings

  1. Vector. The platform's name opens every title bar. Atlas appears nowhere in v3.
  2. Plain group headings (ringed): Work, Run and ship, Observe and govern, Know, AI engineering, Connect. They carry the plain meaning the names lack.

  8. Ask @B at the foot of every screen, Ctrl B.
Plate 02.2 · the map

Names placed on five planes

  1. “Project Cybertron · Vector” (ringed). The only v3 board that names the initiative above the platform.
  2. Experience lists Home, CodeX, Threads, Reviews and @B in Teams.
  3. Control lists Sentinel · policy, Approvals, GroundBridge · gates and model routing.
  4. Execution lists Compute · workspaces and AllSpark; its example step is Ratchet re-running contract tests.
  5. Knowledge lists Matrix · graph and Vault · artifacts (ringed), with Knowledge index, Retrieval and Repositories.
  6. Telemetry and cost lists Optimus Logger and Evals beside Traces, Datasets and Cost.
  7. Bridge, 66 connectors around the planes. One of them is HashiCorp Vault (ringed), a second Vault on the same board.
